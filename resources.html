Tools — CAD software

Learning CAD early is one of the best moves you can make. It shows up in first-year engineering at almost every university, and it's a real, demonstrable skill. All three options below are free for students.

☁
Onshape — Free Plan ⭐ Start here
Cloud-based, runs in your browser on any device — Mac included, no installation. Professional-grade tools for parts, assemblies, and drawings. The free plan is made for students.
Free
🖥
Autodesk Fusion 360
Free for students with a one-year educational licence. More powerful than Onshape — integrates CAD, simulation, and manufacturing tools. Steeper learning curve but more industry-used.
Free (edu)
🟦
Tinkercad
Autodesk's beginner-friendly browser-based tool. Simple drag-and-drop interface. If CAD feels intimidating, start here first before moving to Onshape.
Free

Grade 12 textbooks

The four Ontario courses that matter most for engineering admissions — and the textbooks for each. The Nelson books are the provincial standard. Callan's are student-favourite supplements with 400+ fully solved practice problems per course.

Calculus & Vectors (MCV4U)

📘
Nelson Calculus and Vectors 12
The standard Ontario textbook. Covers limits, derivatives, curve sketching, optimization, exponential/trig derivatives, and full vector algebra — dot product, cross product, projections. Required reading.
Standard
📓
Callan's Grade 12 Calculus and Vectors
Student-favourite supplement with over 400 problems and full step-by-step solutions covering all MCV4U units. Use alongside the Nelson text for extra practice before exams.
Supplement

Advanced Functions (MHF4U)

📗
Nelson Advanced Functions 12
The Ontario standard. Covers polynomial, rational, trigonometric, exponential, and logarithmic functions in depth. Essential prep for university calculus — the foundation everything else builds on.
Standard

Physics (SPH4U)

📙
Nelson Physics 12
The Ontario standard for Grade 12 physics. Covers dynamics, energy & momentum, electric & magnetic fields, wave nature of light, and modern physics — relativity and quantum.
Standard
📓
Callan's Grade 12 Physics
Over 400 problems with full solutions. Great supplement for extra practice on dynamics, electromagnetism, and waves — the units that trip up most students before engineering.
Supplement

Chemistry (SCH4U)

📔
Nelson Chemistry 12
The Ontario standard. Covers organic chemistry, structure & properties, energy changes & rates, equilibrium, and electrochemistry. Required for chemical engineering applicants — worth keeping even if chem isn't your main focus.
Standard
📓
Callan's Grade 12 Chemistry
Over 400 problems with step-by-step solutions. Excellent for organic chemistry and equilibrium practice — the two hardest units for most Grade 12 students.
Supplement

University engineering textbooks

The textbooks you'll actually encounter in first- and second-year engineering at Canadian universities. Buying ahead gives you a serious head start — or at minimum, you'll know what's coming. University libraries carry most of these, so don't buy everything at once.

⚙Mechanical Engineering
Statics & Dynamics
Engineering Mechanics: Statics & Dynamics
Hibbeler (Pearson)
The most widely used statics/dynamics text in North American engineering programs. Clear explanations, extensive problem sets, and strong conceptual foundation. You will almost certainly use this book.
Mechanics of Materials
Mechanics of Materials
Hibbeler (Pearson)
Covers stress, strain, axial loading, torsion, bending, shear, and deflection. Essential for structural and machine design in every mechanical and civil program.
Thermodynamics
Thermodynamics: An Engineering Approach
Cengel & Boles (McGraw-Hill)
The most popular thermodynamics textbook worldwide. Friendly, self-instructive style with real-world engineering examples. Now in its 10th edition — used in mechanical and chemical programs alike.
Fluid Mechanics
Fundamentals of Fluid Mechanics
Munson, Young, Okiishi, Huebsch (Wiley)
Straightforward and well-organized. Covers fluid statics, Bernoulli, control volume analysis, dimensional analysis, and turbomachinery. Standard in mechanical, civil, and chemical programs.
Machine Design
Shigley's Mechanical Engineering Design
Budynas & Nisbett (McGraw-Hill)
Known as "the machine design bible." Found on the desk of nearly every mechanical design engineer. Covers gears, bearings, springs, shafts, and bolted joints in comprehensive detail.
Materials Science
Materials Science and Engineering: An Introduction
Callister & Rethwisch (Wiley)
The standard intro to materials science — metals, ceramics, polymers, composites, and phase diagrams. Used across mechanical, civil, and chemical engineering programs.
🏗Civil Engineering
Statics & MoM
Mechanics of Materials
Hibbeler (Pearson) or Gere (Cengage)
Same foundation as mechanical — stress, strain, torsion, bending, deflection. Civil programs use either Hibbeler or Gere depending on the university.
Structural Analysis
Structural Analysis
Hibbeler (Pearson) or Kassimali (Cengage)
Methods for analyzing beams, trusses, and frames. Essential for structural engineering — the course where structural design starts to feel real.
Geotechnical Eng.
Principles of Geotechnical Engineering
Das & Sobhan (Cengage)
The standard intro to soil mechanics — soil classification, compaction, permeability, consolidation, and shear strength. Required in every civil program.
Concrete Design
Design of Concrete Structures
Nilson, Darwin, Dolan (McGraw-Hill)
Reinforced concrete design — beams, columns, slabs. Uses Canadian concrete design standards. Core upper-year civil text.
Steel Design
Structural Steel Design
McCormac & Csernak (Pearson)
Steel member design — tension, compression, beams, connections. Aligned with Canadian steel design codes used in practice.
⚡Electrical Engineering
Circuit Analysis
Fundamentals of Electric Circuits
Alexander & Sadiku (McGraw-Hill)
The standard first-year circuits text. Covers KCL, KVL, nodal/mesh analysis, transient response, AC circuits, and Laplace transforms. Used at most Canadian universities.
Electronics
Microelectronic Circuits
Sedra & Smith (Oxford)
The bible of analog electronics. Covers diodes, BJTs, MOSFETs, amplifiers, and op-amps. Heavy but essential for anyone going into electronics or hardware engineering.
Signals & Systems
Signals and Systems
Oppenheim, Willsky, Nawab (Pearson)
The standard text for signal processing — Fourier series, transforms, sampling, and filter design. Dense but thorough.
Electromagnetics
Fundamentals of Applied Electromagnetics
Fawwaz Ulaby (Pearson)
More accessible than most EM texts. Covers waves, transmission lines, and antennas with real-world applications — a gentler introduction to a typically difficult course.
Digital Logic
Digital Design
Mano & Ciletti (Pearson)
Boolean algebra, combinational and sequential circuits, VHDL/Verilog introduction. Standard digital logic text across electrical and computer engineering programs.
🧪Chemical Engineering
Mass & Energy Balances
Elementary Principles of Chemical Processes
Felder, Rousseau, Bullard (Wiley)
The first chemical engineering course — material and energy balances around chemical processes. Foundational for everything that follows in the program.
Thermodynamics
Thermodynamics: An Engineering Approach
Cengel & Boles (McGraw-Hill)
Same text as mechanical — chemical programs go deeper into mixtures, phase equilibrium, and chemical potential. One of the most-used engineering textbooks ever written.
Reaction Engineering
Elements of Chemical Reaction Engineering
Fogler (Pearson)
The standard CRE text. Covers reactor design for batch, CSTR, and PFR systems. Well-written and comprehensive — used across Canadian chemical engineering programs.
Heat Transfer
Heat and Mass Transfer
Cengel & Ghajar (McGraw-Hill)
Conduction, convection, radiation, and heat exchangers. Uses the same accessible style as Cengel's thermodynamics book — a natural continuation.
Process Control
Process Dynamics and Control
Seborg, Edgar, Mellichamp, Doyle (Wiley)
Feedback control, PID tuning, and process dynamics. The standard process control text in Canadian chemical engineering programs.
💻Software Engineering
Programming (C)
The C Programming Language
Kernighan & Ritchie (Prentice Hall)
Known as "K&R" — the definitive C programming book. Short, elegant, and foundational for understanding how computers work at a low level. Every software engineer should read this.
Programming (Python)
Introduction to Computation and Programming Using Python
Guttag (MIT Press)
Used in MIT's intro CS course. Teaches computational thinking, not just syntax — a better foundation for engineering programming than most Python books.
Algorithms
Introduction to Algorithms (CLRS)
Cormen, Leiserson, Rivest, Stein (MIT Press)
The algorithms bible. Heavy but comprehensive — every software engineer should own a copy. Used in data structures and algorithms courses at most Canadian engineering schools.
Operating Systems
Operating System Concepts
Silberschatz, Galvin, Gagne (Wiley)
Known as the "dinosaur book." Covers processes, memory management, file systems, and concurrency — the core concepts of how operating systems work.
Discrete Math
Discrete Mathematics and Its Applications
Rosen (McGraw-Hill)
Logic, proofs, combinatorics, graphs — the mathematical foundation of computer science. Used in first-year discrete math courses across Canadian software engineering programs.
✈Aerospace Engineering
Aerodynamics
Fundamentals of Aerodynamics
Anderson (McGraw-Hill)
The standard intro to aerodynamics — incompressible and compressible flow, airfoil theory, and wing design. The foundational aerospace text used in most programs.
Flight Mechanics
Introduction to Flight
Anderson (McGraw-Hill)
Covers aircraft performance, stability, and control. More accessible than Anderson's aerodynamics text — a good starting point for anyone seriously interested in aerospace.
Orbital Mechanics
Fundamentals of Astrodynamics
Bate, Mueller, White (Dover)
Classic, inexpensive Dover paperback on orbital mechanics — the mathematics of spacecraft trajectories. Highly recommended if space interests you.
Propulsion
Mechanics and Thermodynamics of Propulsion
Hill & Peterson (Pearson)
Covers jet engines, rocket propulsion, and gas turbines. The standard propulsion text in upper-year aerospace programs.
Don't buy everything at once. University libraries carry most of these, and many professors post their own notes. If you want one to start with: Hibbeler's Statics for mechanical/civil, Cengel's Thermodynamics for mechanical/chemical, or K&R for software.
