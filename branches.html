Mechanical Engineering
The broadest branch there is.
My focus High demand

If engineering had a "general" option, mechanical would be it. It covers anything that moves — engines, turbines, robots, HVAC systems, medical devices, manufacturing equipment, and more. The reason I'm drawn to it is that it sits at the intersection of physics, design, and real-world problem solving. You're not just running calculations; you're building things that actually exist.

Day to day
  • Designing parts and assemblies in CAD
  • Running stress and heat simulations
  • Working with manufacturing teams
  • Testing prototypes and iterating
  • Writing engineering reports
First & second year courses
  • Statics & Dynamics
  • Thermodynamics
  • Fluid Mechanics
  • Materials Science
  • Calculus & Physics
  • Engineering Drawing / CAD
Career paths
  • Automotive engineer
  • Robotics / automation engineer
  • Aerospace engineer
  • HVAC designer
  • Manufacturing engineer
  • Energy systems engineer
Salary in Canada (2025)
$63K – $102K+
Entry-level to mid-career. Specialists in robotics and automation earn toward the higher end. Top earners reach $130K+.
Strong Canadian universities
University of Toronto University of Waterloo UBC Queen's McMaster
My pick. If you like how things physically work — machines, forces, heat — and want to design things that exist in the real world, mechanical is worth looking at seriously.
🏗
Civil Engineering
The branch that literally builds the world.
High demand Infrastructure

Civil engineers design and build the infrastructure society runs on — roads, bridges, tunnels, dams, water treatment plants, airports, and buildings. It's one of the oldest engineering disciplines and one of the most visible. Every bridge you've crossed and every building you've walked into was signed off by a civil engineer.

Day to day
  • Structural analysis and load calculations
  • Site surveys and inspections
  • Reading and producing technical drawings
  • Working with environmental regulations
  • Coordinating with architects and contractors
First & second year courses
  • Statics & Structural Analysis
  • Soil Mechanics (Geotechnical)
  • Fluid Mechanics
  • Engineering Materials
  • Surveying
  • Environmental Engineering
Career paths
  • Structural engineer
  • Transportation engineer
  • Geotechnical engineer
  • Environmental engineer
  • Urban planner
  • Construction manager
Salary in Canada (2025)
$75K – $115K+
Average sits around $93,900/yr nationally. Strong demand driven by infrastructure investment across Canada.
Strong Canadian universities
UBC University of Waterloo University of Toronto McGill Queen's
⚡
Electrical Engineering
The branch behind everything that runs on power or sends a signal.
Math-heavy High demand

Electrical engineers design and develop electrical systems — from the power grid that lights your city, to the circuits inside your phone, to the sensors in a self-driving car. It's a broad field with a heavy lean toward math and physics, and it bleeds into electronics, computing, and telecommunications.

Day to day
  • Designing and testing circuits
  • Working with microcontrollers and embedded systems
  • Analyzing power flow in large-scale grids
  • Developing signal processing algorithms
  • Writing technical documentation
First & second year courses
  • Circuit Analysis
  • Electromagnetism
  • Linear Algebra & Calculus
  • Signals and Systems
  • Digital Logic
  • Physics
Career paths
  • Power systems engineer
  • Electronics engineer
  • Telecommunications engineer
  • Control systems engineer
  • Embedded systems developer
  • Biomedical engineer
Salary in Canada (2025)
$70K – $164K+
Entry-level around $65K–$85K. Senior engineers with specialization reach $115K–$140K+.
Strong Canadian universities
University of Waterloo University of Toronto UBC McGill McMaster
🧪
Chemical Engineering
Turns raw materials into useful products — at massive scale.
Highest paid Process focus

Chemical engineers figure out how to take a chemical process that works in a lab and make it work in a factory producing thousands of litres a day. They work in pharmaceuticals, food processing, oil and gas, plastics, environmental remediation, and increasingly in clean energy. It's heavily rooted in chemistry and thermodynamics, with a strong process and systems focus.

Day to day
  • Designing and optimizing industrial processes
  • Monitoring temperature, pressure, and flow
  • Running safety and hazard analyses
  • Working on emissions reduction
  • Scale-up from lab to production
First & second year courses
  • General & Organic Chemistry
  • Thermodynamics
  • Fluid Mechanics
  • Mass and Energy Balances
  • Chemical Reaction Engineering
  • Process Control
Career paths
  • Process engineer
  • Petrochemical engineer
  • Pharmaceutical engineer
  • Environmental engineer
  • Biochemical engineer
  • Materials engineer
Salary in Canada (2025)
$91K – $162K+
Highest paid among all major engineering disciplines in Canada on average. Particularly strong salaries in Alberta and BC.
Strong Canadian universities
University of Waterloo University of Alberta UBC University of Toronto McMaster
Note: you'll need Grade 11 and 12 Chemistry for this path. Don't drop Chem early if you think chemical engineering might interest you.
✈
Aerospace Engineering
For people who look at planes and wonder how they actually work.
Specialized Mech overlap

Aerospace engineers design, build, test, and maintain aircraft and spacecraft. In Canada, the field is smaller than mechanical or civil but highly specialized — think Bombardier, Pratt & Whitney, MDA Space, and the Canadian Space Agency. There's a lot of overlap with mechanical engineering in structures, propulsion, and materials, so the two are often paired.

Day to day
  • Aerodynamic modelling and simulation
  • Structural testing of aircraft components
  • Working with propulsion systems
  • Flight simulation and analysis
  • Drone and satellite systems (growing fast)
First & second year courses
  • Statics & Dynamics
  • Thermodynamics
  • Fluid Mechanics
  • Aerodynamics (later years)
  • Flight Mechanics (later years)
  • Materials Science
Career paths
  • Aerodynamics engineer
  • Propulsion engineer
  • Flight systems engineer
  • Spacecraft / satellite designer
  • Drone engineer
  • Aviation safety engineer
Salary in Canada (2025)
$85K – $124K+
Highest salaries in Ontario and Quebec due to concentration of aerospace companies. Top earners reach $200K+ in senior roles.
Strong Canadian universities
Toronto Metropolitan (Ryerson) Carleton McGill Waterloo (via Mechanical) U of T (via Mechanical)
Heads up: many universities don't offer aerospace as a standalone undergrad. Students often enter mechanical engineering and specialize from there. Check if your target school offers a dedicated program before applying.
💻
Software Engineering
Applying engineering principles to building software systems.
Highest earning

Software engineers design, build, and maintain software systems at scale. They apply engineering principles — not just coding skills — to system architecture, reliability, testing, and deployment. The field is massive and ranges from embedded systems (software inside physical hardware) to web platforms to AI systems.

Day to day
  • Writing and reviewing code
  • Designing system architecture
  • Testing, debugging, and deploying software
  • Collaborating with product and design teams
  • Documenting technical decisions
First & second year courses
  • Data Structures & Algorithms
  • Discrete Mathematics
  • Linear Algebra & Calculus
  • Operating Systems
  • Software Design
  • Statistics / Probability
Career paths
  • Software developer / engineer
  • Embedded systems engineer
  • Machine learning engineer
  • DevOps / cloud engineer
  • Cybersecurity engineer
  • Systems architect
Salary in Canada (2025)
$85K – $150K+
One of the highest-paying engineering disciplines. Experienced AI, cloud, and cybersecurity engineers commonly earn over $150K.
Strong Canadian universities
University of Waterloo University of Toronto UBC McMaster McGill
A note from me: I personally moved away from pursuing software engineering at university, even though I enjoy working with computers. That said, it's one of the most in-demand and highest-paying branches out there, so it belongs on this page. If it interests you, look into it seriously.
