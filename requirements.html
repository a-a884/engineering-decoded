Co-op

Waterloo engineering is mandatory co-op — 6 work terms over 5 years. You graduate with nearly 2 years of paid work experience. One of the main reasons students choose Waterloo.

Application deadlines
  • OUAC application: Jan 15
  • AIF + video interview: Jan 30
  • Decisions: Feb–May (rolling)
Supplemental — AIF + Video Interview

Waterloo requires an Admission Information Form (AIF) for all engineering applicants. It covers extracurriculars, work experience, and why you want to study engineering. Some programs also require a short video interview. Your AIF and interview are scored alongside your grades — a strong AIF can meaningfully improve your chances, especially around the 85–88% average range.

Application tip: Waterloo uses a Waterloo Adjustment Factor (WAF) — they adjust your average based on how students from your specific high school have historically performed at Waterloo. Coming from a rigorous school that grades strictly can actually work in your favour. Don't compare raw percentages with students from other schools without accounting for this.
UT
University of Toronto
Toronto, Ontario · #1 ranked engineering in Canada
Most competitive OSP required
Required courses (Ontario)
  • Advanced Functions (MHF4U)
  • Calculus & Vectors (MCV4U)
  • Chemistry (SCH4U)
  • Physics (SPH4U)
  • English (ENG4U)
  • One additional 4U/M course
Competitive average
Mid–high 90s
Minimum cutoff is 70% in required courses, but this is nowhere near competitive. Mean admission average from recent years is in the mid-90s. Aim for 93%+ to be a realistic candidate.
Co-op / PEY

U of T offers the Professional Experience Year (PEY) — a 12–16 month optional co-op in third year. It's competitive but highly regarded by employers. Not mandatory like Waterloo.

Application deadlines
  • OUAC application: Nov 1 (early) / Feb 1
  • OSP early consideration: Dec 1
  • OSP regular deadline: Jan 15
  • Decisions: Feb–May
Supplemental — Online Student Profile (OSP)

The OSP is mandatory for all U of T Engineering applicants. It covers leadership, extracurriculars, and why you chose engineering. Submitting by the early consideration deadline (Dec 1) doesn't improve your chances of admission but may get you a decision earlier. Only first-attempt grades are considered — repeated courses are noted but won't replace original marks.

Application tip: U of T engineering is the most grade-competitive program in Ontario. The supplemental matters, but grades are the primary filter. If you're below 90%, focus on other strong Ontario programs alongside U of T rather than treating it as your main target.

Queen's University
Kingston, Ontario · Strong engineering culture and community
Strong community Co-op available
Required courses (Ontario)
  • Advanced Functions (MHF4U)
  • Calculus & Vectors (MCV4U)
  • Chemistry (SCH4U)
  • Physics (SPH4U)
  • English (ENG4U)
  • One additional 4U/M course
Competitive average
Mid 80s–low 90s
Generally less cutthroat than Waterloo or U of T. Strong applicants typically sit in the 87–92% range. Some programs more competitive than others.
Co-op

Queen's offers optional co-op for most engineering programs. The engineering community and culture at Queen's is one of the strongest in Ontario — something worth considering beyond just grades.

Application deadlines
  • OUAC application: Feb 1
  • Supplemental: varies by program
  • Decisions: Rolling, Feb–May
Supplemental

Some Queen's engineering programs require a supplemental application. Check the specific program you're applying to on the Queen's admissions site — requirements vary between departments.

Application tip: Queen's is often underrated by Ontario students chasing Waterloo and U of T. The engineering program is genuinely strong, the co-op placements are solid, and the community is tight-knit. Worth taking seriously as a target rather than just a backup.
MC
McMaster University
Hamilton, Ontario · Known for problem-based learning
Problem-based Co-op available
Required courses (Ontario)
  • Advanced Functions (MHF4U)
  • Calculus & Vectors (MCV4U)
  • Chemistry (SCH4U)
  • Physics (SPH4U)
  • English (ENG4U)
  • One additional 4U/M course
Competitive average
Mid 80s–low 90s
McMaster makes rolling offers from late February to May. Applying early with strong mid-year grades improves your chances of an early offer.
Co-op

McMaster offers co-op for most engineering programs. The Engineering & Management program (a combined degree) is a popular and distinctive option not offered at most other schools.

Application deadlines
  • OUAC application: Feb 1
  • Offers: Late Feb – May (rolling)
  • By-Selection programs: May
Supplemental

Most McMaster engineering programs require supplementary application information. By-Selection programs (specialized streams selected after first year) have additional requirements. Check McMaster's admissions site for the specific supplemental for your target program.

Application tip: McMaster's rolling admissions means strong mid-year grades can get you an offer before your final marks are in. If you're sitting well in February, this works in your favour. You can apply to up to 3 McMaster programs on one OUAC application.
WU
Western University
London, Ontario · Strong research and campus life
Min 85% avg Co-op available
Required courses (Ontario)
  • Advanced Functions (MHF4U)
  • Calculus & Vectors (MCV4U)
  • Chemistry (SCH4U)
  • Physics (SPH4U)
  • English (ENG4U)
  • One additional 4U/M course
Competitive average
85%+ (mid-year and final)
Western requires a minimum 85% mid-year and final average including prerequisites. This is one of the few schools that explicitly states a minimum. Competitive offers typically go to students in the high 80s–low 90s.
Co-op

Western Engineering offers optional co-op. The Engineering Excellence scholarship stream is available to students who maintain an 80% average in first year.

Application deadlines
  • OUAC application: Feb 1
  • Decisions: Rolling, March–May
Application tip: Western explicitly requires 85% mid-year — not just final. Make sure your mid-year marks in the required courses reflect that before you apply. Falling short mid-year can affect offer timing even if your finals are strong.

British Columbia

Apply via UBC Apply or BCIT · Deadline Jan 15
UB
University of British Columbia
Vancouver, BC · Top 5 engineering program in Canada
Top ranked Co-op available
Required courses (BC students)
  • Pre-Calculus 12 or Calculus 12
  • Physics 12
  • Chemistry 12
  • English 12
Required courses (Ontario students)
  • Advanced Functions (MHF4U)
  • Calculus & Vectors (MCV4U)
  • Physics (SPH4U)
  • Chemistry (SCH4U)
  • English (ENG4U)
Competitive average
90%+
Minimum is around 80% but UBC engineering is heavily oversubscribed. Realistically, aim for 90%+ to be competitive. Selection is based purely on academic standing in the required courses.
How UBC engineering works

All students enter a common first year before choosing their specialization through the Engineering Program Placement process. You pick your branch after first year based on your GPA and preferences.

Co-op

Co-op is available across most UBC engineering programs and is popular with employers in BC's tech and resource sectors.

Application deadline
  • Application deadline: Jan 15
  • No supplemental application required
  • Decisions: Feb–April
Application tip: UBC does not require a supplemental application — it's purely grade-based. This makes your average the only lever you can pull. Strong students from Ontario who apply with 90%+ in the required courses are competitive. If you're an Ontario student seriously considering UBC, make sure your Gr 12 science and math marks are your strongest.

MG
McGill University
Montreal, Quebec · Often called "the Harvard of Canada"
Highly competitive Research-focused
Required courses (Ontario students)
  • Advanced Functions (MHF4U)
  • Calculus & Vectors (MCV4U)
  • Chemistry (SCH4U)
  • Physics (SPH4U)
  • English (ENG4U)
  • One additional 4U/M course
Competitive average (Ontario students)
High 80s–high 90s
Varies significantly by engineering program — some specializations have historically used averages from 85% to above 98%. Check McGill's site for the most recent reference grades by program.
Note for Quebec applicants

Quebec CEGEP students are evaluated on R-score, not percentage averages. Ontario students applying to McGill are assessed on their Top 6 4U/M course average and do not need a CEGEP diploma.

Application deadline
  • Ontario applicants: Feb 1
  • Supporting documents: varies
  • No video interview required
Application tip: McGill considers all academic results including failed or repeated courses. First-attempt marks matter. Unlike U of T's OSP or Waterloo's AIF, McGill's evaluation for engineering is primarily academic — there is no major supplemental essay component for most engineering programs.
UA
University of Alberta
Edmonton, Alberta · Top pick for Chemical Engineering
Chemical Eng Co-op available
Required courses (Ontario equivalents)
  • English (ENG4U)
  • Mathematics with Calculus (MCV4U)
  • Physics (SPH4U)
  • Chemistry (SCH4U)
Competitive average
Mid 80s–low 90s
Generally less grade-competitive than Waterloo or U of T. Strong applicants sit in the high 80s. Chemical Engineering at U of A is particularly strong due to ties with Alberta's energy sector.
Why consider U of A

For Chemical Engineering specifically, U of A's industry connections — particularly in oil, gas, and clean energy — are unmatched in Canada. If that's your branch, it's worth putting on your list even as an Ontario student.

Application
  • Apply directly via ualberta.ca
  • Deadline: March 1 (most programs)
  • Co-op and internship options available
Application tip: As an Ontario student applying out of province, your OUAC transcript is sent directly. U of A evaluates Ontario students on their Top 6 4U/M average using the same required courses as Ontario schools. No supplemental required for most engineering programs.
