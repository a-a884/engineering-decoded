Websites

Free access to actual MIT course materials — lectures, problem sets, and exams across mech, civil, electrical, and chem engineering. Use it to preview what first and second year actually looks like.
All branches

Hundreds of tutorials organized into proper courses — Calculus, Statics, Dynamics, Structural Analysis and more. Free, well-organized, and great for learning ahead or filling gaps.

Tools — CAD software

Learning CAD early is one of the best moves you can make. It shows up in first-year engineering at almost every university, and it's a real, demonstrable skill. All three options below are free for students.

☁
Onshape — Free Plan ⭐ Start here
Cloud-based, runs in your browser on any device — Mac included, no installation. Professional-grade tools for parts, assemblies, and drawings. Built-in learning center with tutorials. The free plan is made for students.
Free
🖥
Autodesk Fusion 360
Free for students with a one-year educational licence, renewable while you're eligible. More powerful than Onshape — integrates CAD, simulation, and manufacturing tools. Steeper learning curve but more industry-used.
Free (edu)
🟦
Tinkercad
Autodesk's beginner-friendly browser-based tool. Simple drag-and-drop interface — good for complete beginners or quick 3D printing prototypes. If CAD feels intimidating, start here first.
Free
📚

Books

Not textbooks. These are reads that will actually make you want to do engineering.

Structures: Or Why Things Don't Fall Down
J.E. Gordon
A surprisingly readable book on structural engineering and materials. Civil and mechanical students both benefit. Covers why things hold together — or don't.
Engineer to Win
Carroll Smith
The engineering behind racing — metallurgy, fasteners, machining — written with a down-to-earth style that makes technical content genuinely readable. Great for mechanical-leaning students.
How Things Work
Louis Bloomfield
Explains the physics behind everyday objects and machines. Builds the kind of intuition that helps in first-year physics and engineering courses.
The Art of Problem Solving (series)
Richard Rusczyk
Not engineering-specific, but builds the mathematical thinking engineers use constantly. Useful if you want to strengthen your problem-solving foundation before university.
